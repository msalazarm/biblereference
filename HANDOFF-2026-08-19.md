### 170 — CLOSED 2026-08-20. Both approved; one refused on evidence; a Latin bug found

Greek scripture — headed by **μέν at 1,538**, which would become **με**. Plus οὐδέν/μηδέν and
the whole -θεν adverb class. Same trade as `προσ -> πατροσ`. Cost of the two kept rules,
measured: 274 colliding forms, occurring **0 times** in our Greek scripture and 21 times in
24 million patristic tokens.

### F1. Every nomina sacra entry that fired, fired wrongly (fold 5)

The expansion exists so a quotation can meet a *manuscript transcription*. This library holds
**printed critical editions**, where an editor has already expanded them — so the contraction
forms never appear as contractions, and are free to collide with ordinary Greek. Eleven did,
on **4,537 words**, catching zero real contractions:

| entry | took | occurrences |
|---|---|---|
| `εσται` → ἐσταύρωται | **ἔσται**, "will be" | **4,412** vs 5 real |
| `θω` → θεῷ | θῶ — *ἕως ἂν θῶ τοὺς ἐχθρούς σου*, Ps 110:1 at Acts 2:35, Heb 1:13, Luk 20:43 | 33 |
| `ιν` → Ἰησοῦν | elided ἵν', and the *hin* measure | 50 |
| `ανουσ` → ἀνθρώπους | ἄνους, "senseless" — all twelve the adjective | 12 |
| `υσ`,`υν` → υἱός/υἱόν | ὗς, the pig: Lev 11:7, 2 Pet 2:22 | 15 |

| `ανων`,`ιηλ` | the names Ἀνών, Ἰήλ | 5 |
| `θυ`,`κυ` | line breaks: `θυ γατέρας`, `κυ κλώσουσιν` | 3 |

The 44 remaining never occur in the Greek corpora, so nothing here can decide them; the

**How it survived an audit.** The table was checked against the lemma lexicon and returned

files ἔσται under `εσταυρωται` and answers "no collision" for `εσται`. *An instrument folded by
the thing it is measuring.* The clean zero was the measurement being blind, not the table being
clean. **Count bare corpus words. Never audit a fold against the lexicon.**

### F2. Editorial marks hid words from every rule (folds 5 and 6)

Each rule in `_greek_word` is a lookup on the whole head, so a mark anywhere made it miss —
and `_WORD_RE` then discarded the mark, so the unfolded spelling entered the index silently.
Fold 5 trimmed the ends (`[οὕτως].`, `—οὕτως`, `[Οὕτως`). Fold 6 was needed because Rahlfs
writes `πολλοί—οὕτως` with no spaces and a mark in the *middle* is not reachable by trimming;
now the word is split on the marks and each run of letters folded, marks kept in place. The
rules moved to `_greek_head` — mapping `_greek_word` over its own pieces recurses for ever on
a piece that is only marks.

## G. 169's seam-noise caution — DISCHARGED, measured (2026-08-20)

`tools/seam_noise.py`. The experiment written down in 169 is run, and the answer is small.

**Why this one is not circular.** The two earlier attempts scored the derived cuts against the
Clementine, which the derivation maximised, and against edge bleed, which it minimises by
construction. This scores them against **the other Old Latin manuscript** — Bianchini numbered
Vercellensis and Veronensis independently of any alignment, and the derivation never saw either.
Throw one manuscript's numbers away, re-derive them by the same procedure, and count the variant
columns it then shows against its neighbour:

```
manuscript / book          verses     true  derived   excess  per verse
  Vercellensis JHN            845    5,526    5,595      +69  +0.08
  Vercellensis LUK            973    8,170    8,241      +71  +0.07
  Vercellensis MAT            997    6,180    6,422     +242  +0.24
  Vercellensis MRK            476    3,806    4,087     +281  +0.59
  Veronensis   JHN            845    5,503    5,475      -28  -0.03
  Veronensis   LUK            974    8,196    8,422     +226  +0.23
  Veronensis   MAT            997    6,206    6,070     -136  -0.14
  Veronensis   MRK            476    3,799    3,914     +115  +0.24
  TOTAL                     6,583   47,386   48,226     +840  +0.13
```

**Deriving the boundaries costs +1.8% variant columns, +0.13 per verse, against a baseline of
7.26 columns per verse.** The alarming reading of the recovery figures — 61-77% exact, therefore
a quarter to a third of verses carry a displaced token — does not translate into columns at
anything like that rate, because a token displaced across a seam usually still aligns.

**Read the spread, not the total.** Per book the excess runs −0.14 to +0.59 columns per verse,
and **two of the eight books come out negative**: the derived cuts agree with the neighbouring
manuscript *better* than Bianchini's own numbers do. So +1.8% is a mean over a real spread, not
a tight estimate, and MRK is consistently the worst on both manuscripts.

**The bias runs toward overstating the harm.** Both manuscripts are Old Latin and share a text
tradition closer to each other than either is to the Clementine, so cutting one to Clementine
boundaries systematically misaligns it against the other. Whatever error that introduces
inflates the excess. **Treat +1.8% as an upper bound on the spurious-column rate in `f` and
`ff2`, not a point estimate** — and note it is measured on `a` and `b`, whose text may be easier
or harder to cut than the two manuscripts that actually need deriving.

### 2MA 1:20 — flagged by `lxx` and `vul` independently, and it is a real seam

Two families flagging one verse is the strongest signal this instrument gives. Reading it, the
verse division genuinely differs — and the decisive evidence is in **Latin**, between two
editions, needing no third language:

* `latvuc` (vul) 1:20 ends `...non invenerunt ignem, sed aquam crassam.`
* `novavulgata` (nvl) 1:20 ends four words earlier at `misit ad ignem.`, and its **1:21 opens**
  `Sicut narraverunt nobis, non invenerunt ignem sed aquam crassam.`

`rahlfs` groups it as `latvuc` does; `peshitta-ot` groups it as the Nova Vulgata does. So
`lxx`/`vul` 1:20 carries a clause that `org`/`nvl` put in 1:21, in a chapter whose verse
**counts** agree perfectly — which is why no count-based check could ever see it.

**It is not a mapping fault and must not be written as one.** `lxx 1:20` = `org 1:20` + the head
of `org 1:21`; `lxx 1:21` = the tail of `org 1:21`. A verse-to-verse mapping cannot express that,
and `1:20 -> 1:20-21` would be wrong in the other direction, swallowing the sprinkling clause
that really is 1:21. **This is a limitation of verse-level mapping, recorded as such.** The
instrument is right and there is nothing to correct.

Worth noting what it means that this is the *first* thing the corrected witnesses found: with
brenton and dra out of the way, the flags start pointing at the text instead of at the numbering
of an English translation.
